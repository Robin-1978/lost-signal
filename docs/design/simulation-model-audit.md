# Simulation and autonomy model audit

This document is the release claim boundary for Lost Signal. A passing scenario is gameplay evidence. It is not, by
itself, evidence that a physics, sensor, or autonomy model is correct. Every claim below is classified as:

- **Verified**: an analytic result, independent geometric/numerical oracle, or cross-layer invariant is executable.
- **Internally consistent**: the interfaces and implementation agree, but parameters or the abstraction still need
  external evidence.
- **Calibration required**: only measurements from identified hardware, HIL, or a held-out real dataset can establish
  quantitative sim-to-real accuracy.

The release build is verified with GCC 13. `tools/build.sh` compiles a C++20 `std::atomic::wait/notify_all` probe and
selects a capable compiler; it refuses to reuse a build directory configured by a different compiler. Clang 18 is a
separate compatibility check. The workstation's unversioned GCC 9 is not a supported MuJoCo toolchain.

## Reference contracts

The implementation follows these external semantics without adopting ROS 2 or another project's middleware:

- [MuJoCo modeling: contact parameters](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/modeling.html#contact-parameters) for
  `solref`, `solimp`, friction dimensions, and compliant contact interpretation.
- [MuJoCo computation](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/computation/index.html) for generalized state,
  constraint forces, and integration semantics.
- [REP-103](https://www.ros.org/reps/rep-0103.html) for SI units and right-handed robot axes: X forward, Y left,
  Z up.
- [sensor_msgs/Imu](https://docs.ros.org/en/noetic/api/sensor_msgs/html/msg/Imu.html) for body-frame angular velocity
  and linear-acceleration measurement semantics. Lost Signal additionally states explicitly that the ideal
  accelerometer truth is specific force.

These references define meanings. They do not validate Lost Signal's numerical parameters.

## Audit matrix

| Subsystem | Claim and independent evidence | Status | Quantitative limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Time integration | MuJoCo owns rigid state; every public step is split into fixed 1 ms internal steps; force is held over substeps rather than treated as an impulse; state is refreshed after the final substep. Step-end pose, force/mass response, and deterministic replay fixtures execute these contracts. | **Verified** | A 1 ms step does not prove convergence for every future stiff model. Each new contact or actuator model needs a step-halving study. |
| Coordinates and frames | World/base/link transforms use right-handed SI conventions. Free-joint local/world angular velocity conversion, joint axes, pivots, wheel axes, and frame composition have independent tests. | **Verified** | Imported meshes still require validated asset-axis metadata. |
| Contact | MuJoCo Newton solver, elliptic cone, positive-format `solref`, `solimp`, and per-material friction are wired to native contacts. Normal and tangent impulses are observed from the solver. | **Internally consistent** | The solver is a compliant constraint model, not an identified rubber tire. Contact time constants, damping ratios, friction, rolling loss, and tread geometry use typical defaults until measured. |
| RO-1 differential platform | Two articulated driven wheels and one articulated crowned caster form a physical three-point support. Wheel efforts pass through motor plants, joints, and contact; body velocity is never assigned from a command. Static load, slope, traction, braking, spin, arc, curb, roughness, and jitter fixtures execute. | **Verified** for topology, signs, kinematics, force path, and tested invariants; **calibration required** for numerical fidelity | Tire/caster compliance, wear, backlash, battery voltage sag, motor temperature, and manufactured mass distribution are not identified. |
| RO-2 Ackermann platform | Front steering joints obey Ackermann inner/outer geometry; rear wheel drive and two passive service-brake torque capacities act through wheel contact. Steering, high-speed cornering, wet/dry braking, split friction, stopping distance, and no-reverse-through-zero brake tests execute. | **Verified** for geometry and causal brake/contact path; **internally consistent** for dynamics | No ABS, hydraulic circuit, combined-slip identified tire, aero load, suspension, or thermal brake model. Speed and brake defaults are representative, not a real vehicle certificate. |
| RO-3 tracked platform | Six driven road wheels create a skid-steer vehicle whose commanded efforts and contact forces are physical. | **Internally consistent** | This is explicitly a multi-wheel skid-steer approximation. It does not model a continuous belt, sprocket engagement, track tension, shoe-ground pressure distribution, or thrown-track failures. |
| RO-4 swerve platform | Four drive joints and four steering joints use measured joint feedback, rate/effort-limited control, module kinematics, and physical contact. Kinematic inverse/forward invariants and dynamic maneuver/braking fixtures execute. | **Verified** for module geometry and control/data path; **calibration required** for transient fidelity | Steering backlash, suspension, tire relaxation, compliance, and identified module inertia are absent. Visible oscillation must be fixed in control or identified mechanics, never by pose damping. |
| RO-5 mecanum platform | Four wheel joints use the mecanum Jacobian. A provider-side diagonal creep force is computed from contact-point velocity and previous-step MuJoCo normal load and is bounded by local friction. | **Internally consistent** | The creep coefficient is a method constant, not an identified roller model. Individual rollers, bearing loss, roller impacts, and compliance are not represented. |
| RO-6 manipulator | Yaw plus planar 3R FK/IK, joint limits, measured-joint feedback control, effort saturation, gravity feedforward, MuJoCo articulated links, and end-effector agreement have executable fixtures. Removing effort exposes gravity. | **Verified** for the declared compact geometry and tested force path; **calibration required** for hardware fidelity | No flexible links, gearbox backlash, cable dynamics, force/torque sensor, grasp contact model, or identified payload inertia tensor. |
| Motor and steering plants | Commands produce bounded normalized duty, lumped force/torque response, joint effort, and encoder feedback. Stall force and free speed define the mechanical curve; `energy_scale` is isolated to mission energy estimation. Ackermann steering angle remains a steering angle across LSRI and does not silently become yaw rate. | **Verified** for represented units, saturation, signs, and closed-loop wiring; **internally consistent** for plant shape | There is no explicit bus voltage, winding current, back EMF, inverter/gear efficiency, regeneration, battery, temperature, or magnetic saturation model. The energy score is a configured proxy, not calibrated electrical consumption. |
| Payload mass | Runtime payload changes preserve generalized pose/velocity. Root-link and payload mass are combined at their first-moment COM; diagonal inertia is shifted to that COM with the parallel-axis theorem. MuJoCo bodies use a general mass block so runtime inertial-origin changes are supported. A force-through-body-origin oracle proves that an offset COM creates the expected moment without moving `base_link`. | **Verified** for vertical COM, mass and diagonal-inertia propagation; **calibration required** for a real load | Payload is a lumped, centred mass. Lateral/longitudinal placement, a full non-diagonal inertia tensor, attachment compliance and load motion require an explicit payload body or richer inertial contract. |
| Planar LiDAR | Production simulation raycasts the 3D MuJoCo world, filters self geometry by collision layer, models scan timing, moving actors, range/intensity, and separate static-reference truth. Thin-pole, wall, occlusion, min/max range, dropout, intensity, motion, pitched-ground, and shifted-reference fixtures execute. | **Verified** for ray geometry and time semantics; **calibration required** for sensor statistics | Beam divergence, multi-return, material wavelength response, rain/fog and correlated noise are not modeled. |
| 16-line LiDAR | One spinning scan produces ring, azimuth, point time, intensity, and 3D coordinates. Dynamic actors use a closed finite-cylinder intersection including side, top, and bottom caps. Analytic side/cap/miss tests and the production RO-1 cloud chain execute. | **Verified** for declared scan pattern and geometry; **calibration required** for a specific unit | Vertical angles and noise are typical component data. Firing schedule is deterministic interleaving, not a vendor packet emulator. Performance gates apply to the shipped 360×16 at 10 Hz profile. |
| IMU | Root acceleration plus `alpha × r` and `omega × (omega × r)` produces sensor-point acceleration; gravity is removed to obtain specific force; angular velocity and force are transformed into `imu_link`. Static gravity, lever arm, and a 90° mounted-IMU oracle execute. Bias random walk and white noise are deterministic under seed. | **Verified** for rigid-body truth, axes, and installed-frame expression | Accelerometer constant bias and device delivery latency are not yet configurable in content. Noise density/bandwidth and bias parameters require sensor data. |
| LSRI runtime | Sample and receive time, sequence, source, frame, schema version, TTL, bounded queues, lifecycle, health, replay, mixed native/sim endpoints, out-of-order rejection, and watchdog commands are tested. | **Verified** for protocol behavior | A watchdog safe stop requests zero motion or channel safe values. Actual stopping distance remains a chassis/controller/contact property and has not been certified for real hardware. |
| Localization | Encoder/IMU observations feed the same estimator in simulation, replay, and future hardware. Calibrated IMU mounting, quaternion angular-rate integration, bounded gravity correction, timestamp order, deterministic replay, dropout, and slip-observer behavior have fixtures. Estimated roll and pitch are available to sensor projection without simulator body truth. | **Internally consistent** | No GNSS, scan matching, SLAM loop closure, calibrated covariance-consistency benchmark, or real bag validation. IMU/motion transport latency needs an explicit queue before latency robustness can be claimed. |
| Static and observed maps | LSRM keeps occupancy, elevation/material layers, semantic features, revisions, checksums, and immutable runtime snapshots separate. Elevation survives local-map composition. Local observed occupancy ray-clears stale cells; attitude-aware LiDAR projection rejects returns consistent with mapped ground. Simulation body pose and actor labels do not enter ROBOT perception. | **Verified** for package/coordinate/ownership and represented projection contracts; **internally consistent** for perception quality | No production SLAM provider, survey compiler, scan matcher, or large tiled map backend yet. Costmap inflation remains a circular-footprint approximation. Ground rejection depends on map/elevation alignment and a configured tolerance rather than calibrated uncertainty. |
| Grid A* | Eight-connected cost search returns forward `RoutePoint` values with geometric headings. Diagonal edges require both adjacent cardinal cells to be free, preventing zero-width corner cutting. | **Verified** for grid optimality contract and corner rule | A point/grid plan relies on prior footprint inflation and cannot express Ackermann curvature. |
| Hybrid A* | Bicycle-model primitives carry searched body heading and gear and are sampled for occupancy/terrain feasibility. The returned terminal point is the searched state inside goal tolerances; no unchecked exact-goal edge is appended. Multi-waypoint planning continues from the actual terminal pose. | **Verified** for the declared primitive model and route continuity | It has no analytic Reeds-Shepp connector and checks an inflated point path rather than a polygon footprint. Primitive sampling resolution remains part of the safety case. |
| Local rollout planning and validation | Differential, Ackermann and holonomic commands are restricted to the next decision interval's reachable velocity window; the longer prediction horizon cannot authorize an immediate terminal-speed jump. Differential DWA uses separate normal drive and normal brake rates, keeps direction-specific reachability explicit, and checks wheel-space reachability. Its stability-aware emergency-stop rate is a separate validation contract; zero safe authority rejects motion instead of disabling that contract. Numerically equivalent DWA scores use an explicit stable secondary order. Candidate generation rejects physical overlap while the independent validator owns uncertainty margin, minimum-risk escape, swept collision, terrain, response delay, dynamic load projection and emergency-stop constraints. The load projection is checked against the convex hull of the actual rolling contacts, so RO-1 uses its three-point triangular support instead of an invented rectangle. If no candidate survives, the stop request is staged from measured motion; differential/tracked stopping is rate-limited in wheel space and its deceleration ceiling uses loaded COM, asymmetric support bounds and mapped longitudinal grade. | **Verified** for represented fixtures, including asymmetric reachability, zero brake authority, floating-point tie arbitration, triangular-support geometry, staged high-speed stop/turn, high-COM orchard stopping, cross-slope motion and approaching-actor escape transitions | Footprints are circular. Prediction is deterministic multi-mode constant velocity/reflection, not a calibrated probabilistic predictor. Ackermann and holonomic candidate generators still use a symmetric nominal acceleration limit. The support/COM envelope is a quasi-static effective-gravity/ZMP approximation with an engineering margin. It is not a suspension model, load-transfer-ratio estimator, tire relaxation model or active rollover controller, and it still requires real-platform calibration. |
| Dynamic prediction | `predict_frames` builds every frame from one immutable input list and emits each object's nominal and alternate modes in stable order. All swept/interpolated consumers compare the same index between adjacent frames. | **Verified** for forecasts produced by the built-in predictor | Frame identity is positional rather than carried as a typed track/hypothesis ID. Third-party forecast providers must preserve order; a future API should make identity explicit before out-of-process prediction is supported. |
| Behavior and mission | ROBOT behavior derives stop/wait/proceed decisions from route geometry, conflict zones, observations, prediction and chassis caps. Canonical, shortcut and continuous-fillet paths are evaluated as separate timed candidates. Simulation mission code owns authored world progression and truth actors; it does not grant collision-free motion. DWA and the trajectory validator continue checking after behavior commits. | **Verified** for dependency direction, fillet geometry, bounded-track reassociation and tested crossing transitions | The current deterministic policy is not a general behavior-tree framework. Authored conflict-zone evidence does not validate every encounter topology or an inferred-road-user predictor. |

## Release gates

A release candidate must run, in this order:

1. GCC 13 clean headless build and the complete CTest suite.
2. ROBOT-only dependency/build checks, content/schema validation, locale validation, and generated-artifact checks.
3. Deterministic headless runs repeated with identical scenario, seed, configuration, build revision, and algorithm
   version; compare the full result artifact and key trajectory metrics.
4. Graphical smoke tests for the recommended experience and proving ground, including input responsiveness and clean
   shutdown.
5. ASan/UBSan after functional gates pass.

The project may claim a credible, inspectable simulation and autonomy learning platform within the tested scope. It
must not claim validated sim-to-real transfer, certified safety, identified tire behavior, or real-sensor accuracy
until calibration data, held-out validation, and HIL or field error bounds are published.
